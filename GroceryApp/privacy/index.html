1. Quick Summary

PantryRun is a privacy-first grocery list app. Your grocery lists are stored locally on your device and encrypted with XChaCha20-Poly1305. We don't run central servers that see your data — there are no accounts and no sign-ups. When you use family sharing, your lists sync through a self-hosted relay server that you run and control. Your list data is end-to-end encrypted, and neither we nor anyone operating network infrastructure can read it. This version of PantryRun sends no analytics, no crash reports, and no telemetry to anyone.

2. What We Collect

We collect nothing. No data from PantryRun reaches servers we operate or any third party that keeps it. The data the app handles, and where it lives:

  • Grocery list data — sensitive text fields such as item names, notes, and list names are encrypted locally. Quantities, categories, identifiers, and timestamps remain readable in the local database; notification history may contain readable names. The database file relies on your device's storage protection. When family sync is on, list content travels to your own relay server as end-to-end-encrypted ciphertext.
  • Device pairing key — a randomly generated public key used for family pairing. It is sent only to your own relay server during enrollment. Not linked to your identity; not a hardware or advertising ID.
  • Family member display names — names you choose for yourself within the app. Encrypted at rest, synced only as ciphertext.
  • Barcode numbers — only if you turn on optional barcode lookup, scanned barcode digits are sent to the Open Food Facts public database to fetch the product's name. The service also sees the network IP address used for the request. See Optional Features.

We do not collect: your real name, email address, phone number, location, contacts, advertising identifiers, analytics events, crash reports, or any browsing history.

3. How Your Data Is Stored

  • On-device: Grocery data lives in a WatermelonDB database with sensitive text fields encrypted using XChaCha20-Poly1305; the whole database is not encrypted by the app. Sensitive keys (encryption keys, pairing codes, your recovery material) are stored in your device's secure credential storage via expo-secure-store.
  • In transit: Family sync uses WebSocket connections to your relay. Sync payloads are end-to-end encrypted — the relay forwards and briefly stores opaque ciphertext it cannot read.
  • On your relay: Your self-hosted relay does store data so that offline devices can catch up: encrypted list updates (automatically deleted after 30 days), device enrollment records (which expire 30 days after a device's last connection), and — if you use flyer scanning — nothing: flyer images are processed in memory and discarded. Everything retained is either ciphertext or a random public key. Because you run the relay, this data never leaves infrastructure you control.
  • Your keys, your data: Encryption keys exist only on your devices and in your 12-word recovery phrase. There is no server-side reset. If you lose all of your family's devices and the recovery phrase, your data is permanently unrecoverable — by design.

4. Family Sharing & Relay

PantryRun's family sharing works by pairing devices via QR code. Paired devices sync through a WebSocket relay server that you run yourself (we provide the open-source relay software; this version of the app has no hosted relay option).

The relay server sees only: your device pairing key (a random string), connection timestamps, and encrypted blobs it cannot read. No grocery item names, list contents, or personal information are visible to the relay. The encrypted blobs it stores expire automatically after 30 days.

5. Optional Features

Price Lookups (Opt-In)

If you enable the price lookup feature, PantryRun matches your grocery list against prices stored locally on your device — prices read from flyers you scan and prices you enter yourself. In this version of the app, your item names are never sent to any server for price lookup. Your shopping habits are not tracked or stored anywhere off your device. You can disable this at any time in Settings.

Flyer Scanning (Opt-In)

If you photograph a store flyer to extract prices, the photo is stripped of location and camera metadata (EXIF) on your device and then sent to your own relay server for extraction, after which it is discarded. Unlike your grocery lists, flyer photos are not end-to-end encrypted — your relay can see the flyer image contents (store and product names and prices printed on the flyer). Because you host the relay, the photo stays inside infrastructure you control. This feature only runs when you tap the flyer-scan camera button and can be disabled in Settings.

Barcode Scanning & Lookups (Opt-In)

Camera access is used to scan product barcodes for adding items. Camera frames are processed in real-time on your device and discarded — no images are captured, stored, or transmitted. To turn a barcode into a product name, PantryRun sends the barcode number and nothing else to the Open Food Facts public food database. No account, device identifier, or list data accompanies that request, and we retain nothing from it. Barcode lookups are off by default: the app asks for your consent the first time you open the scanner, and you can turn them off anytime in Settings. (A USDA FoodData Central fallback exists in the code but is inactive in this version — no API key is configured.)

Voice Input

This version of PantryRun has no voice feature: the app never requests microphone access and processes no audio. The "voice input" button opens an on-device text box where you can type or paste a phrase like "2 apples and milk" to add several items at once. If a real voice feature ships in a future update, this policy will be updated first.

Push Notifications

PantryRun uses expo-notifications for local reminders (e.g., shopping reminders). Notification data stays on your device. We do not send remote push notifications through any third-party service.

6. Analytics & Crash Reporting

This version of PantryRun sends no analytics, no crash reports, and no telemetry to anyone. The app contains no advertising or analytics SDKs, and its crash-reporting module ships unconfigured and never activates. If a future update adds optional crash reporting, it will be disclosed here, reflected in the app-store privacy labels, and controllable in the app before it sends anything.

7. Your Rights & Data Deletion

Because PantryRun has no accounts and stores your data locally:

  • To delete local data: Use Settings → Delete All Data in the app. After confirmation, the app attempts to erase the local grocery database, settings, and keys this install can address, including the master encryption key and device keypair. If a step fails, some data or keys may remain; the app reports errors it receives so you can retry. Some secure-storage cleanup failures may not be reported; completion is not a guarantee that every key was erased. This does not erase copies on other family devices or your relay. Uninstalling is not a guarantee that secure-storage entries are erased on every platform.
  • To clear only price data: Use the "Clear Local Prices" button in Settings.
  • To leave a family: Unpair your device in Settings. Copies of your family's encrypted updates may remain on your own relay server for up to 30 days, after which the relay deletes them automatically; you can also delete them immediately since you administer the relay. They are ciphertext throughout.
  • About unreachable copies: Secure device storage cannot enumerate every stored key, so recovery or passkey entries for forgotten identities may survive a wipe. The app attempts to delete known keys, but cannot guarantee that every forgotten entry becomes undecryptable. Relay ciphertext is not erased by local deletion and may remain readable to other family devices or anyone holding the recovery phrase. Your relay administrator must erase relay copies separately if immediate deletion is needed.
  • To export your data: Your grocery data is in a local SQLite database. We plan to add a CSV export feature in a future update.

There is no server-side copy of your data for us to delete: this version of the app talks only to the relay server you host yourself. Questions about any of this: privacy@pantryrun.app.

8. Children's Privacy

PantryRun is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children. If you believe a child has provided us with personal information, please contact us and we will delete it.
